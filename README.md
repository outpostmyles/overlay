# Overlay

Overlay is a sports-betting research engine that treats de-vigged prediction-market prices (Polymarket, Kalshi) as the sharp fair line and grades everything against it: sportsbook odds, player props, and its own forecasts. Its defining feature is that it keeps score on itself: every prediction is frozen before the game and auto-graded after, so the results below are what the ledger says, not what I claim.

It started as a World Cup 2026 tool, was re-architected mid-tournament into a multi-sport platform without disturbing the live board, and now runs MLB, NHL, the NFL and Power 4 college football around the clock on a $6/month server with $0 in API spend.

## Results (all from the self-grading ledger)

**World Cup 2026, complete.** 27 knockout forecasts, each locked ~75 minutes before kickoff with the de-vigged market line frozen at the same instant as the benchmark:

- The independent model picked 23/27 winners (85.2%) vs the market favorite's 19/27 (70.4%), but lost on calibration: Brier 0.446 vs the market's 0.418 (-6.7% skill, closer than the market on only 7 of 27). Better at picking, worse at pricing.
- The skill gap moved from -19.9% at n=9 to -6.7% at n=27. Still a market win at a small sample, which is the finding: the project's thesis is that the de-vigged market is the sharp reference, and the ledger confirmed it against its own model.
- A separate bet ledger holds 112 logged picks, 71-33-8 settled. Only the 23 moneyline picks carry a captured closing line; props, team totals and parlays are logged without one, and that capture has a bug described under limitations, so no closing-line-value figure is quoted here.

**MLB, live and unattended since July 19.** Anchor-only by design: no model, because the WC ledger just demonstrated the market wins the main line. Each game locks a full sheet (moneyline, total, first-5-innings call, the 3 most competitive player props) before first pitch and grades off free box scores. **As of 2026-10-01: 807 games settled** across the regular season from 2026-07-19 to 2026-09-30, plus 3,334 graded prop, total and first-five legs, for 4,141 auto-graded predictions in all. The 807 are a near-complete census of games starting before 21:00 ET: a cache bug, since fixed and disclosed in full under limitations, stopped later first pitches from ever locking.

The headline is a null, and that is the point: market favorites won **476 of 807 (59.0%)** against **462.7 expected** from the market's own de-vigged probabilities, z = +0.95. On the standard 2-outcome scale the Brier is **0.238** (95% CI +/- 0.006), against a 0.242 perfect-calibration floor and 0.250 for a coin flip. The prices do almost exactly what they claim to do. A correctly functioning anchor should look like this, and no edge is claimed.

The per-leg results are the better demonstration, because the ledger corrected itself. At n=197 in August, totals appeared to beat their own frozen line by 3 percentage points. At n=3,334 that gap is gone: player props 1,210/2,330 (51.9% against 52.8% predicted), totals 192/351 (54.7% against 52.8%), first-five 320/653 (49.0% against 48.2%). Every one is inside noise, all |z| < 1. The totals and first-five samples are smaller than the game count because of two upstream rewordings, quantified under limitations. Legs are compared against their own frozen lines rather than against 50%, because they are deliberately selected near a coin flip and 50% was never the right baseline.

Three reads from the same data carry across sports. Backing every MLB favorite would have returned +2.6% at the fair price but about -1.5% after Kalshi's fee and a 1-cent spread: the prices were fair, so the costs were the only edge in play, and they ran the other way. Kalshi's props showed no under bias: across 2,356 MLB props, unders hit 48.6% against 48.7% priced, so any prop edge lives at a sportsbook and Kalshi's fair line is the yardstick to measure it. And favorites priced at 70% or more (-233 or shorter) won 24 of 25 across MLB and the World Cup against 18.5 expected (z = +2.5). That matches the well-known favorite-longshot bias, but it is 25 games and was noticed before it was tested, so the boards now track favorites by price live, pooled across every sport, rather than claim it; the football seasons will add hundreds of heavy favorites.

**NHL, live since 2026-10-06.** Same anchor-only design, same ledger, built on the MLB adapter: each game's de-vigged moneyline and main total lock ~75 minutes before puck drop and grade off the free ESPN final. Every input was probed live before it was written. Kalshi's moneyline settles on the official final, overtime and shootout included, and ESPN credits a shootout winner with one goal, so the final never ties and the total counts the shootout goal exactly as the totals market settles. The 1st-period market is left out because its book is thin (a 9-cent spread) and honestly priced near a three-way coin flip, which is not a sharp line; the puck line waits for spread support, and player props for Kalshi to post them. No NHL games have settled yet, so there are no NHL results to report.

**NFL, live since 2026-10-06.** The same anchor-only ledger, built around how the owner bets: each game locks its de-vigged moneyline, main spread, main total, and a 24-prop sheet over the five props that matter (passing, rushing and receiving yards, receptions, and anytime touchdown). Each type gets its own slots, filled by role: both starting quarterbacks, the lead backs, the featured receivers, and the likeliest scorers. The first version instead took the 15 most competitive lines across all eleven Kalshi prop series, which handed six of Thursday's fifteen slots to passing attempts and completions and left touchdowns nearly absent, since their odds sit far from a coin flip; the six unused series were dropped. Within a type, one line per player: a ladder's rungs (25+, 40+, 50+ receiving yards) are the same bet several times. Everything grades off the free ESPN box score. Grading follows Kalshi's own settlement rules: a player who never takes a snap voids, while one snap means he is graded on what he recorded, zero included. Because ESPN lists a player only in the stat groups where he recorded something, anyone appearing anywhere in the box score counts as having played. On the Oct 4 slate the extracted player rushing yards summed exactly to ESPN's team totals for all 28 teams. No NFL games have settled yet.

**College football (Power 4 and Notre Dame), live since 2026-10-06.** Moneyline, main spread, and main total for any game involving an SEC, Big Ten, Big 12 or ACC team, or Notre Dame, with membership taken from ESPN's 2026 standings. The scope also solves liquidity: across all 215 games Kalshi listed, the median moneyline spread was 20 cents, which is noise rather than a sharp line, while every in-scope game in game week was at 3 cents or tighter. A 10-cent gate keeps any thinner book off the ledger until it tightens. Teams are keyed on the school name so a Power 4 team's weekly opponent needs no hand-written alias, and when Kalshi's listing date disagrees with ESPN's (it dated four Oct 17 games "Oct 16"), the game takes ESPN's date, which is safe only because a college team plays once a week. No college games have settled yet.

**Research layer (NFL and college football), live since 2026-10-06.** Beside the market line, every football game carries a separate Research %, frozen at the same lock and graded the same way, so the ledger can show whether published betting research improves on the crowd. One rule decides what may move it: a factor adjusts the number only where studies show the betting market itself misprices it. That means the favorite-longshot bias on moneylines (favorites at 60% or more gain 1.0 to 1.5 points, about half the gap measured both by this ledger and by a 2025 study of Kalshi's own trades) and wind and rain on NFL totals (half the gap in a published study: unders hit 55% at 8-11 mph over 1,194 games and 59% at 12-14 mph over 499). Everything the market already prices stays out of the number, because adding it again would count it twice: a starting quarterback on the injury report, rest, night games for West Coast teams, big college spreads, and weather on college totals. Those are shown on each card and graded in a factor study with their own hit rates and z-scores, so the record decides over time which ones earn weight and whether the adjusting ones keep theirs. The inputs are free: venue, roof, injuries and a DraftKings line from ESPN, plus the kickoff-hour forecast from Open-Meteo. Each card also shows the worst price that still clears a 2% edge over the Research % (4% for props, and more while a quarterback's status or a missing college availability report leaves a question open), and scores DraftKings' price against it. No football games have settled yet, so there is no Research result to report.

Spreads, in both football boards, come from Kalshi's one-market-per-line books ("Dallas wins by over 7.5 points"; its no side is Tampa Bay +7.5), de-vigged line by line, with the main line locked and graded off the final margin. The covering team is read from the ticker code, since the labels word teams differently by sport. On its first live run the silent-join watchdog flagged 354 NFL and 803 college spread markets joining nothing: the de-vig step priced only a whitelist of market types, and spreads were not on it. A test now asserts that every type the parser emits gets priced.

## How it works, briefly

Free public APIs (Kalshi, Polymarket, ESPN, Open-Meteo) feed a normalize → de-vig → grade pipeline. De-vigging uses the power method (SciPy `brentq` solves for the exponent that makes outcome probabilities sum to 1). Forecasts write to a SQLite ledger with a strict lifecycle (pending → locked at T-75, before lineup feeds → settled), and aggregate metrics are withheld until n ≥ 8 so a couple of games can't masquerade as a verdict. A 300-second in-process heartbeat keeps four live systemd services (MLB, NHL, NFL, college football) grading with no browser attached, beside the finished World Cup archive.

## Decisions that required judgment

- **Kill your own model when the data says so.** The forecast ledger exists to test the model against the market. It lost. MLB therefore ships with *no* model (the de-vigged line is the forecast) instead of a rebuilt one. The architecture encodes the finding.
- **De-vig validity over coverage.** Kalshi lists totals as one market per line; the lines of one game are not mutually exclusive, so de-vigging across them is statistically invalid. Each line is parsed as its own two-way book. Same discipline filters placeholder quotes (a far-out first-5 market quoting every leg equal de-vigs to exact thirds; that is noise, and it never locks).
- **Refactor a live system with proof, not confidence.** The multi-sport seam landed under a record-and-replay golden harness: all network inputs pickled once, time frozen, and the full pipeline replayed before and after, with byte-identical output required, while the WC board was in production.
- **Settlement is where money-adjacent code dies quietly.** Baseball added failure modes soccer never had, each with a mechanism: doubleheaders get distinct identities from the ticker's embedded start time (and honestly never lock rather than guess which game is which); a rainout voids, never loses; a game postponed after its lock can only grade against a same-date final; an adversarial review caught that it would otherwise silently settle against the makeup game and poison the record.
- **Adversarial audits over self-belief.** Multi-agent review passes ran against each major change and found real bugs before they cost data: forecasts for post-midnight-UTC kickoffs being voided hours before their lock window; a date-only market twin from a second source falsely flagging every MLB game as a doubleheader (nothing would ever have locked); an ESPN sweep wasting 40 requests per refresh (now 3). One they did *not* catch in time is the more useful entry: the same UTC-date-boundary hazard survived inside the kickoff cache, which expired its entries by kickoff time but its "already fetched" dates by scoreboard date. Those two calendars disagree for any first pitch after 20:00 ET, so late games lost their kickoff at UTC midnight and were never re-fetched, and 149 forecasts were voided instead of locked before a later audit found it. It is fixed with a regression test, and the damage is quantified below rather than quietly dropped, because a ledger that keeps score on itself has to report its own instrument failures too.
- **Cost as a constraint, not an afterthought.** The deployed server runs keys-blank, hard-guaranteed $0, because the free core (de-vig, ledger, grading) is the product. Paid feeds (Anthropic, The Odds API) fire only on manual actions, behind debounces and credit floors.

## Honest limitations

Nothing here is a profit claim, and the ledger explicitly must not be used to retune the model. Two leg types also have gaps caused upstream, and both are fixed as of 2026-10-06. On Aug 19 Kalshi reworded its market titles (a totals title went from naming the game to just "Over 8.5 runs scored"), so the totals join, which read the game out of that title, silently matched nothing: 455 of the 807 games locked after that date and have no totals leg. From Sep 13 to Sep 28 Kalshi labeled the first-five tie "Tie first 5 innings" instead of "Tie", which the parser read as a third team, so 154 games have no first-five leg. No error was raised either time. Both joins now take the game from the market's ticker codes, which did not change, rather than from its prose title, and a watchdog logs any market type that is listed but joins to no game. A second log line flags any listed game that ESPN cannot place, which is what caught the college dates above on the first run. A material coverage caveat: of the 956 MLB games the ledger opened a forecast on, 149 (15.6%) never locked and were voided instead of graded, and that attrition is not random. It is a hard cliff at a 21:00 ET first pitch (92 graded and 0 dropped in the 20:00 hour, then 5 against 99 at 21:00 and 0 against 40 at 22:00), which removes West Coast home dates specifically: games involving one of the seven Pacific-time clubs are graded at 64.1% (250 of 390) against 98.4% (557 of 566) everywhere else, and the worst-hit franchises lose a third to half their season (LA Angels 31 of 63 graded, Athletics 36 of 64, Seattle 36 of 63, Arizona 40 of 64, LA Dodgers 40 of 63, San Diego 43 of 65, San Francisco 43 of 64). The loss is steady across July, August and September (16.2%, 15.1%, 15.8%), so it was a standing defect, not a start-up artifact. The cause is now identified and fixed: the in-process kickoff cache pruned its entries by the kickoff's UTC date but its "already fetched" dates by the US-local scoreboard date ESPN was queried for, and those disagree for every first pitch from 20:00 ET on, so a late game's kickoff was evicted at UTC midnight and then never re-requested, leaving no kickoff for the entire lock window. It is specifically *not* an ESPN gap: the scoreboard does return those games under the queried date. The headline null survives this, but as a bound rather than as a clean sample: the 250 graded West Coast games lean slightly *more* favorite-friendly than the rest (61.6% actual against 58.7% market-expected, versus 57.8% against 56.7% elsewhere) and are marginally better calibrated (Brier 0.231 against 0.241), and for the 149 missing games to push z = +0.95 outside ±1.96 they would have to finish above a 69.8% or below a 29.8% favorite-win rate, outside every subgroup of n >= 50 in the graded data, which span 52% to 68%. The 149 stay void and are not retroactively settled: no line was ever frozen for them, so there is nothing to grade, and manufacturing results from finals would invent a record the system never made. Read the 807 as a census of games starting before 21:00 ET plus two-thirds of West Coast baseball, not as a sample of all MLB. The WC sample (n=27) is small enough that its model-vs-market gap is indicative, not conclusive. Brier figures use this project's internal 3-outcome sum convention (range 0 to 2), so they are comparable across rows here but not directly against a textbook 0-to-1 Brier. Closing-line capture shipped late in the WC (n=6 there) and runs for every MLB game. Over the 807 regular-season games, locking 75 minutes early cost nothing detectable. Kalshi prices are re-fetched on every 5-minute tick until first pitch, and on 495 of the 807 (61%) the de-vigged line did not move at all in that final 75 minutes; when it did move, 72% of the time it was by exactly one cent tick (0.5 or 1.0 probability points). On the 312 games where it moved, the closing line was no better calibrated than the locked one (Brier 0.2420 against 0.2424). An earlier version of this README called the unchanged games a measurement artifact; checking the capture path showed it was not, with one exception. For first pitches at 20:00 ET or later, the kickoff-cache bug described above also cut the close capture short at 20:00 ET, so those games' unchanged rate (79%, against 52% before 17:00 and 64% from 17:00 to 19:59) is partly mechanical; that is fixed as of 2026-10-06. The afternoon-to-evening gap below 20:00 is not explained by that bug, and its cause is not yet identified. Separately, the bet ledger's own closing capture had no kickoff cutoff, so it kept overwriting during play: 10 of its 23 priced rows sit pinned at 0.989 and all 10 won, and the closing price correlates +0.36 with the result where the pick price correlates -0.06. That is in-play leakage. The capture now stops at first pitch and is keyed per game (fixed 2026-10-06), but the pre-fix history cannot be repaired, so no CLV number is published for it. Doubleheader games never lock (by design, pending per-event kickoff data), and a known consequence is that they can never settle either. Polymarket's MLB game markets aren't ingested yet (their named-outcome format needs its own parser); PrizePicks is bot-blocked and treated as permanently optional. Player-prop fair values come from thin books; they are displayed, not bet. Single-user tool: no auth, one shared dashboard per sport.

## Stack and scale

Python / FastAPI / httpx (async), NumPy + SciPy, SQLite, vanilla-JS SPA (no build step). About 13,900 lines of Python, JavaScript, CSS and HTML across backend, tests, and frontend (roughly 10,600 excluding blank lines, comments and docstrings; 82 tracked files), **216 tests, all passing** (odds math, settlement edge cases, the golden harness, sport adapters, kickoff-cache date boundaries, closing-line cutoffs, real Kalshi payloads in each wording it has used, football box-score grading and spreads, and the research layer's adjustments, grading and migration), 54 commits. Deployed on a single DigitalOcean droplet: nginx + five systemd services, per-sport caches, sport-scoped ledger in one database, running keys-blank at $0 API spend.

## Setup

```bash
./run.sh                      # venv + deps + http://localhost:8000 (World Cup archive)
SPORT=mlb PORT=8010 ./run.sh  # the live MLB board
```

All API keys are optional; with none set it runs entirely on free sources. Key configuration is in `.env.example`; always-on deployment (systemd + nginx, keys-blank) is in `DEPLOY.md`.
